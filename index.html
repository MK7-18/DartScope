Calibration modesAutonomous: no taps, any board colours (red/green, black/white, blue and so on). It takes several frames, aligns them and keeps what stays still, so a moving hand or a slightly shaky phone is fine. It finds the board by its alternating sector pattern, reads the printed numbers (digit matching) to find the 20, and checks itself on the ring pattern and the bull. It only accepts when sure; otherwise it opens the point editor with its proposals.
Auto: finds the red and green rings in the picture, fits an ellipse for the board plane, then you tap the 20 segment once. Fast but approximate (it ignores strong perspective). Needs good light, the whole board in frame and no other red or green objects. Always check the overlay.
Manual: you tap four reference wires on the outer double ring (20|1, 6|10, 3|19, 11|14) and then the bull. This builds an exact perspective mapping and reports the error in mm. Slower, most accurate.
Both modes let you nudge every point with the on-screen arrows (or keyboard arrows, Shift = bigger steps, Enter = confirm) and check it in the magnifier before confirming. Recalibrate whenever the phone or board moves.
